# CortenForge — Mission

*Established 2026-06-03. Re-aimed 2026-09-18.*

> **Machines the people who feed us can fix themselves, fuel themselves, and own outright.**

CortenForge is a software development kit for mechatronics and simulation — geometry, parametric design, meshing and fabrication, rigid- and soft-body physics, control and reinforcement learning, sim-to-real. **The kit is the product.**

To prove the components are real and compose end to end, we build the hardest useful thing we can with them: **a family of hydrogen farm vehicles for the upper Midwest, and the fuel chain that powers them.**

- **Tractor** — for work.
- **Truck** — for haul.
- **Trike** — for leisure.

All three run on hydrogen made from the farm's own wind. The fuel chain is modelled, not assumed: if it does not close on one farm's wind, the simulation says so.

## The three constraints

*Fix it yourself, fuel it yourself, own your data.* Constraints with gates, not a slogan.

| | gated by |
|---|---|
| **Fix it yourself** | part count, distinct fastener types, no proprietary diagnostic |
| **Fuel it yourself** | acres per season from that farm's measured wind year |
| **Own your data** | no telemetry, as a property of the firmware, not a promise |

## Why this first

The adoption logic holds: robotics introduced into economic precarity reads as threat. Robotics that demonstrably helped the people who feed the country reads as something else entirely. Same machine, different reception, depending on what came before it.

## The question

**How many acres per season can one farm run on its own wind-derived hydrogen?**

Every crate contributes to that number, and it is checked against public ground truth — Nebraska Tractor Test drawbar and slip, NREL wind, SAE J2601, DOE H2A — so it is validated rather than plausible.

⚠ The hard part is not hydrogen. It is **terramechanics**: drawbar pull and wheel slip on deformable soil, which the tractor's number runs straight through and which no off-the-shelf engine models.

## Also live

Person-specific assistive robotics — stroke recovery, assistive living, sports equipment — runs on the same substrate and is sequenced behind the farm, not abandoned. Contact, actuation, control and fit-to-a-body are the same components whether the body is an operator or a patient.

## The breadth

The farm is one path through the kit, not its edge. The same components serve robotics and control, soft robotics, biomechanics, generative design and digital fabrication, scanning and reverse engineering, custom-fit products, and embodied-AI research — anywhere a physical thing has to be designed, simulated and made.

We measure success not only by the demonstration, but by how readily someone reaches for a CortenForge component to build something we never planned.

## Honest constraints

Simulation-only. No hardware, no capital, nothing built or driven. A model that matches Nebraska drawbar data is a validated model, not a machine — and hydrogen at 700 bar carries safety and regulatory weight that code does not clear.

We sequence by quality and ceiling, never by demo speed, and hold *validated against measurement* as the line between a demonstration and a tool.
